02 — BUSINESS QUESTION

Who may leave,
and where should a team look first?

In a subscription business, a single churn number hides useful detail. The analytical task is to separate lifecycle effects, compare customer groups consistently, and translate a risk signal into a focused investigation.

THE SCENARIO

A fictional subscription service

This self-initiated portfolio case uses a generated dataset representing monthly customer activity, subscription plans, billing and support signals. It is designed to demonstrate the analytical workflow, not to represent a real company or a production model.

  • Population: 8,420 generated accounts across four segments
  • Time span: 18 simulated months; dashboard view focuses on the latest 12
  • Business goal: make retention performance explainable and actionable
DECISION TO SUPPORT

Move from monitoring to a useful next step

A useful report should help a retention or customer-success team answer three questions without overstating what the model knows:

  • Where is churn changing? Separate overall movement from segment-level behavior.
  • Which customers deserve a closer look? Compare lifecycle, plan and recent engagement signals.
  • What should happen next? Use a transparent queue for human review and measure follow-up outcomes.
03 — ANALYTICAL APPROACH

Five deliberate steps,
one connected workflow.

Each step keeps business definitions, data quality and decision use connected. The project is intentionally small enough to explain in an interview and broad enough to show the full analytics lifecycle.

STEP 01 / FRAME

Define the outcome

Agree on the churn event, observation window and unit of analysis. Separate customer loss from temporary inactivity and document the reporting grain.

STEP 02 / PREPARE

Build a trustworthy base

Use Python to profile and validate synthetic records, standardize dates and categories, check duplicate keys and test the monthly customer snapshot.

STEP 03 / MODEL

Shape the analytical model

Use SQL to create reusable customer-month facts and conformed plan, date, segment and region dimensions. Keep definitions consistent across reports.

STEP 04 / ANALYZE

Explain patterns

Compare monthly churn, cohort retention and MRR exposure by tenure and segment. Treat associations as investigation clues, not proof of causation.

STEP 05 / ACT

Design the decision loop

Prioritize a human-reviewed customer-success queue, record intervention eligibility, then monitor contact, save and later retention outcomes.

05 — METRIC DEFINITIONS

Shared definitions
before shared dashboards.

Clear metric contracts prevent teams from comparing different denominators under the same label. Definitions below are project assumptions for the synthetic demo.

Monthly customer churnCustomers with a qualifying cancellation during the month ÷ customers active at the start of that month.
Logo retentionEligible starting customers still active at period end ÷ eligible customers at period start.
Cohort retentionCustomers from a shared start-month cohort who remain active after N full months.
MRR at riskCurrent recurring revenue associated with accounts above a review threshold; an exposure estimate, not guaranteed loss.
